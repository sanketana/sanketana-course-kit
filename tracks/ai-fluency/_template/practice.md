# Practice — Asking a Better Question

Extra projects for when the prompt log is finished and there's time left. Pick one; they're
independent. Each is harder than the classwork on purpose.

## Practice Projects

### 1. The standard for something else

Pick something you'd actually ask an AI for this week — a revision summary, an idea for a
present, an explanation of something you didn't follow in class. Before you ask, write three
lines saying what a good answer would look like.

**The stretch:** ask, then rank the answer against your own three lines and say which line it
missed. If it met all three, your standard was too easy — sharpen it and go again.

**Done when:** you have a standard, an output, and a one-sentence verdict that points at a
specific line.

### 2. Write the bad answer on purpose

Take Plan B from `assets/sample-outputs.md` — the polished, useless one. Work out what prompt
would produce it.

**The stretch:** reverse-engineering a bad output is harder than improving a good one. You have
to work out what was *missing* from the question, not what was wrong with the answer.

**Done when:** you can send your prompt and get back something recognisably like Plan B, and
name the one thing you deliberately left out.

### 3. The same question, three tools

Ask the same well-built prompt of two or three different AI tools your family has approved.

**The stretch:** rank the answers against one standard, not three. Then answer the harder
question: did the differences come from the tools, or from the prompt being loose enough that
they could each read it differently?

**Done when:** you can point at one difference caused by the tools and one caused by your
prompt.
