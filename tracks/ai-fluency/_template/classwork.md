# Lesson 2 — Asking a Better Question

## Lesson Theme

An AI tool answers the question you asked, not the one you meant. Today you'll watch the same
task produce three different results from three prompts, and learn to predict the gap before
you hit enter.

## What You'll Build

A prompt, refined three times, that produces a study plan you'd actually follow — and a one-paragraph
note on what each version got wrong.

## Tools Used

- Any chat AI your family has approved (we use Claude in class)
- `assets/prompt-log-study-plan.md` — your log for today

## What You'll Learn

- Vague prompts get generic answers; the fix is detail, not politeness
- How to predict a prompt's weak spot before sending it
- Ranking outputs against a standard you wrote yourself

## Prompt Lab

### Round 1 — the obvious prompt (5 min)

Type: *"Make me a study plan."* Before sending, write in your log: what will this get wrong?
Send it. Were you right?

### Round 2 — add what you know (5 min)

Rewrite with your real subjects, the exam date, and the hours you have. Predict again. Send.

### Round 3 — add what you want (5 min)

Tell it what a good plan looks like *to you* — short sessions, one hard subject per day, whatever is true.
Predict, send, compare.

## In Class

### Build the standard (8 min)

Write three lines: what makes a study plan good *for you*. This is your standard. Keep it; you'll use it below.

### Tool Judgment (12 min)

Open `assets/sample-outputs.md` — three plans written for a student like you. Rank them against your
standard. For each, one sentence: what it gets right, what it gets wrong.

Then rank your own three rounds the same way. Did the best prompt make the best plan?

### Refine once more (10 min)

Take your best prompt and fix its one biggest weakness. Send. Log it.

## Ethics Check

Your plan has your subjects, your exam dates, maybe your weak spots. Who else can see what you typed?
Does that change what you'd put in a prompt?

## Reflection

- What did round 1 get wrong, and could you have predicted it?
- Describe a prompt you sent this week that would have been better with one more sentence.
- The tool wrote the plan. Who decided what "good" meant?

## Key Takeaways

- Detail beats politeness. The tool can't read the sentence you didn't write.
- Predict the weak spot before sending. If you can't, you don't understand the task yet.
- Your standard comes first; the output is judged against it, not the other way round.
